Structural Design of Shanghai Tower for Wind Loads 
X. Zhaoa*, J. M. Ding1 and H. H. Suna
aArchitectural design & research institute of Tongji university (Group) Co., Ltd., 200092, Shanghai, China 
Abstract 
Nearby Jin Mao tower and the World Financial Center, the 632-meter, 121-story mixed-use Shanghai tower will be 
the tallest super tall tower in the new Lujiazui Finance and Trade Zone of Shanghai. Due to the super tall height, 
curved façade and spiraling form of the Shanghai tower project, wind is the dominant lateral load and governed many 
aspects of the structural design. To precisely determine the design wind loads, a detailed wind climate study was 
conducted. The influences of typhoon events and typhoon profiles were considered in the wind climate study. Due to 
its long structural period, the across-wind load of the Shanghai tower project is prominent. Aerodynamic optimization 
studies were conducted to reduce the correlation of vortex shedding along the building height, and thus reduce the 
across-wind building response. The optimization results show that the across wind load can be effectively reduced 
with certain building configuration. Detailed wind tunnel studies, including HFFB and HFPI studies, high Reynolds 
number tests and aeroelastic model tests, were conducted to accurately capture the wind load on the building. The 
wind load parameters, such as the Shape factor, Strouhal number and the terrain type, were obtained and derived from 
the wind tunnel studies. The wind load and wind induced structural responses obtained from the wind tunnel studies 
were then compared with those calculated based on Chinese load code. The design criteria for the ultimate limit 
check and serviceability limit check of Shanghai tower for wind loads were discussed. Finally, the building vibration 
under wind load and the related human comfort problem is addressed. Different human comfort criteria are compared 
and the building acceleration results from both the wind tunnel tests and code-based calculation are discussed.

Introduction 
Nearby the Jinmao tower and the Shanghai World Financial Center tower, the Shanghai tower is a 
580,000 m2 multi-use development located in the new Lujiazui Finance and Trade Zone of Shanghai. The 
structural height is 580m and the architectural height is 632m. The building consists of 121 floors of 
office and hotel space, with additional 5 stories above for MEP functions. The building has been designed 
as a soft vertical spiral to contain the vertical city slowly rises towards the sky. 
Figure 1 Lujiazui CBD after the completion of the Shanghai tower 
Figure 2 Geometry of the Shanghai tower 
Due to its super height and the twisting and rotating façade (figure 2), the wind loading is one of the 
controlling factors of the structural design of Shanghai tower. Due to its close distance from the sea, the 
influence of typhoon on the design wind speed of Shanghai area was thoroughly studied. A series of wind 
tunnel tests were conducted in RWDI’s wind tunnels located in Guelph city, including high-frequency 
force-balance tests, high-frequency pressure-integration tests and aeroelastic model tests. The model scale 
of the above tests is 1:500. A set of high-frequency force balance tests was conducted for the 
aerodynamic optimization studies. In order to further investigate the influence of Reynolds number on the 
wind loading, a large scale model (1:85) was built and tests in the 9mx9m aeronautic wind tunnel to 
investigate the high Reynolds effects. 
This paper summarizes the above wind engineering studies and compared the wind loading obtained 
from wind tunnels and those predicted using Chinese codes. The main wind loading parameters, including 
the shape factor, Strouhal number and terrain type, were discussed. The structural design for the wind 
loading, design criteria and the design wind parameters were then addressed. Those wind loading 
parameters used in the Jinmao tower and Shanghai world financial center were also listed and discussed. 
Finally the wind induced vibration and related human comfort issues were discussed. Different human 
comfort criteria were addressed and the building acceleration obtained from both wind tunnel tests and 
code-based calculations were compared. The possible building vibration control system for this project 
was also discussed.

Wind Climate Study 
The ratio between the surface hourly mean wind speed and gradient height hourly mean wind speed 
is 0.54 in Chinese load code GB50009-2001. In the super tall building design, such as Jinmao tower and 
the Shanghai world financial tower, it was believed that the wind field model developed by Georgiou 
(1985) was employed. In the Georgiou’s wind field model, the surface wind speed ratio is about 0.68 for 
standard terrain roughness B. Based on the wind field model proposed by Kepert (2001), a new wind field 
model was developed by Vickery (2009) using typhoon profile data recorded by a series dropsonde tests. 
According to Vickery’s wind field model, the surface wind speed ratio is about 0.60 for standard terrain 
roughness. The gradient wind speed predicted by the new wind field model is about 13% higher than that 
predicted by the old wind field model. Table 1 lists the 100 year return period 10 minute mean gradient 
wind speed used in the Jinmao tower, Shanghai world financial center (SWFC), Shanghai tower and the 
code.

Aerodynamic Optimization 
Due to high design wind speed and relatively low overall stiffness, tall buildings are susceptible to vortex 
induced vibration. For super tall buildings, the across wind load due to vortex shedding is even higher 
than the along wind load. Figure 3 show the vortex shedding of the typical section of Shanghai tower. The 
consistence of the vortex shedding along the building height will be disturbed if the building shape 
changes. The effective building shape modification methods include softened corners, tapering and 
setbacks, varying cross section shape, adding spoilers and porosity or openings in the building elevation 
(Miyashita et al. 1993). 
Figure 3 Vortex shedding of the typical section of the Shanghai tower 
The main features of the Shanghai tower building façade include the twisting angle, building orientation 
and the shrink ratio of building plan along the building height. The high-frequency force-balance (HFFB) 
wind tunnel tests were conducted for the parametric analysis on twisting angle and building orientation. A 
rectangular shape building with the same plan shrink ratio was applied as the reference. The building 
model with twisting angle of 100, 110, 120 and 180, and with orientation angle of 0, 30 and 40 were 
investigated. Table 2 compares the base shear reactions obtained for the schemes with different twisting 
angles and orientations (RWDI, 2009).

As shown in figure 4, the wind speed and wind pressure profile simulated in the wind tunnel tests 
are very close to those specified in the Chinese code below the height 200m. The wind speed and wind 
pressure profiles of code become larger than those of wind tunnel tests. According to the analysis results 
based on wind tunnel tests, the shape factor of Shanghai tower is about 0.95 and the Strouhal number for 
Shanghai tower is about 0.16. 
To investigate the impact of high Reynolds effects on the building wind load, a set of 1:85 scale 
wind tunnel tests were conducted in the 9mx9m aeronautic wind tunnel of the National Research Council 
of Canada. The maximum test wind speed is about 50m/s. Three wind conditions were investigated, say

Structural Design for Wind Loads 
The structural design of Jinmao tower is based on the past Chinese load code GBJ9-87. The code 
GBJ9-87 defined 100 years return period wind load, which is related to the gradient wind speed of 
57.4m/s, was applied for the strength design of Jinmao tower (Zhang and Shi, 2000). The wind 
engineering study results was applied in the stiffness design and human comfort evaluation of Jinmao 
tower. For the stiffness design, the situations of both early Pudong area and developed Pudong area are 
considered. The structural deformation results under the wind load of 30 years return period with 1.5% 
damping ratio, 50 years return period with 2.5% damping ratio and 100 years return period with 3.5% 
damping ratio are considered. The human comfort evaluation of the Jinmao tower based on the wind load 
of both 1 year return period and 10 years return period. In the structural design of the Shanghai world 
financial center tower (Wang et al. 2007), the wind climate study predicted 200 years return period wind 
load was applied for the strength design. The corresponding gradient wind speed is about 46.3m/s. The 
wind load was further amplified by 1.1 times, which is related to the increase of wind speed by

The wind-induced vibrations can be suppressed by changing structural parameters. The building’s 
acceleration response can be halved by double the structural mass and stiffness. However, if the 
supplementary damping devices were used, the acceleration can be halved by using about 2~3‰ of the 
building mass (Tamura et al. 1995). To lower the maintenance cost, a pendulum configuration like the 
Taipei 101 was proposed in this project. Due to the long structural period, a long suspension length is 
required. A stacked double-hung pendulum method is applied to reduce the required space. 
For the Chinese code based calculation, the damping ratio of 2% is applied. 10 years return period wind 
load is employed in Chinese code to assess the human comfort. It is assumed that the typhoon effect is 
considered for the 10 years return period wind load in Chinese code and the structural damping ratio can 
be increased to 5% with the installation of TMD. The acceleration results with and without TMD system 
are listed in table 9.

Conclusions 
The wind climate study indicates that, the influence of typhoon should be considered in the typhoon 
prone area. Due to the development of the field measurement technique in the past decades, the state-of-
the-art typhoon model can more accurately reflect the typhoon profile. The wind loading is the governing 
factor for the structural design of super tall buildings. Through aerodynamic optimization on the building 
shape, the wind load can be effectively reduced. The building shape modification methods commonly 
become the resource of the design inspiration of the architects. For super tall buildings like the Shanghai 
tower project, the wind loading can only be accurately obtained through thorough and detailed tests and 
analyses. Different wind tunnel test methods, such as the high-frequency force-balance method, high-
frequency pressure integration method, aeroelastic model test and high Reynolds number test were 
conducted in different design stage of the Shanghai tower project. The wind load results obtained from 
the wind tunnel study and those obtained by Chinese code based calculation are compared to obtain 
further understanding on the wind load. 
By comparing the design wind loads of the Shanghai tower, Jinmao tower and Shanghai world 
financial center, one can find the development of the engineering technology with the ongoing of the 
times. It was also revealed that, although the value of certain wind load parameter differs, the final overall 
wind loads for the three super tall building do not differ too much due to the impact of engineer judgment.

For the Shanghai tower project, although the stiffness design result is controlled by wind load, the 
strength design results of critical structural members are mostly controlled by moderate earthquake due to 
the performance based seismic design requirements. 
The analysis and control of wind-induced vibration is one of the critical design topics for super tall 
buildings. The most important factor for the evaluation of the wind-induced vibration is to choose 
appropriate wind load level. The evaluation results for 1 year wind load and 10 year wind load are quite 
different. For the Shanghai tower project, under 1 year return period wind load, and with the TMD system 
installed on the building top, the highest building habitability standard can be satisfied.
