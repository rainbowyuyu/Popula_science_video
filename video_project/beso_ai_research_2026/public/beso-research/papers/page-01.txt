Summary paragraph (~195 words) 
Physical engineering design must satisfy simultaneous constraints across fluids, solids and 
stability while remaining manufacturable and cost-effective. Agentic artificial-intelligence 
systems have automated parts of scientific discovery, yet complex multi-physics design still relies 
on human experts to stitch together CAD, mesh generators, structural solvers and aero-hydro-
servo-elastic codes whenever geometric, numerical or limit-state failures occur. Here we show 
that an agentic orchestrator coupling large language models to deterministic engineering 
backends—the AI Engineer—can autonomously traverse this loop for a 20-megawatt floating 
offshore wind foundation. An internal Automated Reviewer, calibrated on eleven real floating 
projects, terminates exploration only when a candidate clears a pre-validated composite score 
with no weak sub-dimension; the same candidate subsequently obtained Approval in Principle 
from the China Classification Society. Relative to a human-optimized national demonstration 
baseline, the AI-generated design reduced steel intensity and unit capital cost while satisfying 
coupled limit-state checks. The result establishes verification-closed automation of certifiable 
engineering design as a practical paradigm, with accountability and auditability treated as first-
class constraints. 
Introduction 
From towering wind turbines to floating platforms that enable deep-water renewable generation, 
the performance of engineered systems is set by their physical design. Unlike software, physical 
artefacts must simultaneously satisfy fluid dynamics, solid mechanics and structural stability 
under uncertain operating conditions, while remaining manufacturable and economical. For 
centuries this process has depended on expert intuition and labour-intensive iteration. As margins 
tighten and multi-physics coupling intensifies, the limits of purely human-centric design become 
clear.
