candidate) and enables high-throughput screening before any AIP submission. 
Third-party Approval in Principle 
As gold-standard calibration, we submitted the single highest-scoring candidate (**S = 91**, 
grade A) to CCS for AIP, with a dossier matching the CCS checklist (design basis, arrangements, 
stability, hydrodynamics, global strength, mooring and codes list). Appraisal proceeded through 
preparation, application, independent review and certification. CCS issued three rounds of 
clarification—on design rationale and static performance; strength and fatigue under combined 
loads; and station-keeping under extremes—after which all items closed and the AIP certificate 
was issued. Because the orchestrator retains WebSocket logs, SHA-256 integrity manifests and 
solver input decks, each clarification could be traced to the emitting computational step, 
accelerating responses. 
Two implications follow. First, clearing **S ≥ 85** predicted successful AIP for the selected 
candidate. Second, AIP is a one-time calibration of the internal gate, not a daily objective 
function: subsequent candidates can be screened at negligible marginal cost while remaining 
aligned with third-party practice. AIP does not replace detailed design, fabrication inspection or 
type approval; those remain out of scope. 
Discussion 
The AI Engineer shows that agentic loops coupled to deterministic solvers can move from 
natural-language requirements to third-party-certifiable artefacts. Unlike open-ended discovery 
agents judged by novelty and later laboratory validation, this system operates in a **verification-
closed** regime: every proposal is scored against physics and codified limit states, and failures 
trigger deterministic replanning rather than textual caveats. The routine terminal condition is the 
Automated Reviewer gate; AIP sits outside the daily loop as external proof that the gate tracks 
professional judgment. 
A two-stage cascade resolves the cost–credibility trade-off: cheap internal scoring across 
thousands of candidates; rare external certification for the selected concept. Transfer to other 
domains requires deterministic solvers, thresholdable codes and a reference fleet for calibration—
