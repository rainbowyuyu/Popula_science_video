diverge from solver conventions, geometry interferes after parametric updates, or a structural 
unity check exceeds 1.0, humans diagnose and restart the toolchain—a loop that leaves large 
regions of the design space unexplored (Fig. 1a). Generative models trained on static datasets lack 
live access to governing physics and tool error codes; reinforcement learning and Bayesian 
optimization, while effective for scalar tuning inside a fixed pipeline, struggle with heterogeneous 
software ecosystems and discrete failures spanning geometry, numerics and limit states. 
What is missing is an agentic orchestrator that reads solver diagnostics against codified limit 
states and replans—remeshing, relaxing increments or resetting the design domain—without 
human attention at every failure. Here we introduce The AI Engineer, which couples large 
language models with schema-validated deterministic backends in a closed loop (Fig. 1b). 
Natural-language requirements are parsed into structured specifications; the system then executes 
topology optimization (bi-directional evolutionary structural optimization, BESO, with CalculiX), 
parametric upscaling, particle-swarm size optimization under aero-hydro-servo-elastic load cases 
(Zwind), and automated reporting. Exploration terminates not at local convergence alone, but 
when an Automated Reviewer calibrated on real regulatory benchmarks judges the candidate 
certification-ready. Formal Approval in Principle (AIP) from a classification society is used once 
as an external calibration of that gate, not as a per-run objective. We demonstrate the framework 
on a 20 MW FOWT foundation relevant to China’s dual-carbon goals; the AI-generated design 
obtained China Classification Society (CCS) AIP and surpassed a human-optimized baseline on 
key economic metrics while satisfying limit-state constraints. 
Autonomous design beats human baseline 
We benchmarked the AI-generated foundation against the “TuQiang” baseline—the world’s first 
20 MW floating wind demonstration unit, developed over two years by a large university–
industry consortium. Figure 2a compares the three-dimensional configurations. The AI candidate 
reduces steel intensity from 278 to 255.5 t·MW⁻¹ (8.1%) and unit capital cost from ¥19,800 to 
¥18,197 kW⁻¹ (8.1%), remaining below the national demonstration target of 300 t·MW⁻¹. These 
gains are not obtained by sacrificing dynamics or integrity. Tower natural frequencies lie outside 
the 1P and 3P excitation bands of the 20 MW turbine (Fig. 2b); platform motions under power 
production and extreme sea state (DLC 6.1) are comparable to or lower than the baseline (Fig. 
2c,d); maximum strength unity check remains below 1.0, fatigue damage stays within a 25-year 
life with Design Fatigue Factor 3.0, and mooring peak tensions remain below breaking strength 
(Fig. 2e). Thus the system converged to a design that is lighter, cheaper and compliant with 
coupled limit states. 
Calibrated internal certification gate 
Submitting every exploratory candidate to a classification society is infeasible. The Automated 
Reviewer therefore scores each candidate on five dimensions—capacity, steel intensity, unit cost, 
constructability and fatigue life—using piecewise linear functions calibrated on a reference fleet 
of **11** real floating wind projects (WindFloat and WindFloat Atlantic counted as two entries).
